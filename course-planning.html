Pedagogical Planning & Learning Design

Progressive, project-anchored delivery. The eight units are sequenced as one continuous build-up rather than isolated topics: static markup and layout (Units 1–2) → client-side interactivity (Unit 3) → DOM manipulation (Unit 4) → JavaScript objects and ES6 (Unit 5) → server-side logic (Unit 6) → database integration (Unit 7) → security hardening (Unit 8). Students carry a single evolving web application across the semester, adding one capability per unit, so every new concept is applied immediately to a real, growing codebase instead of a throwaway exercise.

Paired theory–lab sessions. Each unit's theory session (concept walkthrough + live coding demo) is followed within the same week by a guided lab session where students implement the concept on their own project, with faculty circulating for one-to-one troubleshooting — keeping the gap between "taught" and "practised" as short as possible.

Formative checkpoints. End-of-unit lab evaluations and short code walkthroughs map directly to CO-1 through CO-5, surfacing misconceptions before the next unit builds on top of them, and feeding into the continuous assessment components on the Theory and Practical assessment pages.

Shared faculty ownership. Ravi Patel and Dhaval Patel co-own theory delivery, each maintaining their own week-by-week Lesson Planning Sheet below; Priyam Mehta owns lab planning, keeping the practical schedule aligned to the same unit sequence so lecture and lab hand off cleanly.

Inclusivity & Learner Support Strategy

Levelling the entry point. Students arrive with uneven exposure to the stated pre-requisites (basic programming, HTML/CSS). Units 1–2 open with a quick bridging recap, backed by remedial material on the Material page, so no one is left decoding syntax while the class moves on to concepts.

Differentiated pacing in labs. Every lab task has a core requirement everyone must clear, plus optional stretch goals drawn from the Content Enrichment Plan above. Faster learners can push into those extensions while faculty use the same slot to give struggling students direct one-to-one time on the core task.

Peer support. Lab seating deliberately mixes stronger and struggling students so doubts get a first pass through peer explanation before they're escalated to faculty, backed by an open Google Classroom thread for questions outside lab hours.

Multiple ways to revisit content. Every taught concept has a corresponding entry on the Material page, so a student who misses a session or needs to re-learn at their own pace isn't dependent solely on in-class notes.

Flexible accommodation. Students with a documented disability, medical leave, or genuine scheduling conflict are offered make-up lab slots and extended assignment windows on request, coordinated directly with the concerned unit's faculty owner rather than a rigid blanket policy.

Course committee. A course committee — comprising the teaching faculty, the department, and student representatives — meets each semester to consolidate all three feedback streams and decide on concrete revisions: rebalancing unit hours, adjusting Content Enrichment Plan topics, or refining the Inclusivity & Learner Support Strategy above. Approved changes are carried forward into the next semester's Lesson Planning Sheet and Unit-wise Plan.
